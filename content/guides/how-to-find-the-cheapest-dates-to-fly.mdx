The fastest way to save on a flight is to be flexible about the date. A fare calendar shows the lowest recent price for each day, so you can see the cheap days at a glance instead of searching one date at a time.

## Reading the calendar

Every route page on FarePockets, like [New York to Los Angeles](/flights/new-york-to-los-angeles), has a "Cheapest days to fly" section covering the next three months:

- **Cheapest dates found** lists the five lowest fares across all three months. Start here.
- **The cheapest day in each month** is highlighted, and lower-than-usual fares are tinted, so cheap stretches stand out.
- **A price on a day** is the lowest one-way fare found in recent searches for that departure date.
- **A day without a price** just means nobody searched it recently. It can still be cheap.

## Turning a cheap date into a booking

1. **Pick a highlighted day,** or any day near one. Cheap fares often cluster in the same week.
2. **Click the day.** It opens live results for that exact date on our partner Aviasales, where you compare airlines and booking sites.
3. **Check the final price.** Calendar prices come from recent searches and can change. Confirm the total, including bags and seat fees, before you pay.

## Tips that make the biggest difference

- **Fly midweek when you can.** In our data, Tuesday departures averaged 17% below a route's typical fare. See [the cheapest day of the week to fly](/guides/cheapest-day-of-the-week-to-fly).
- **Price each direction on its own.** The calendar shows one-way fares, so look up your return separately. Two one-way tickets are sometimes cheaper than a round trip.
- **Search the whole city, not one airport.** Including nearby airports can turn up cheaper flights. Here is [how metro airport codes work](/guides/search-the-whole-city-metro-airport-codes).
- **Quieter routes have fewer prices.** On smaller routes the calendar may only show a handful of days. Click any day you are interested in to see live fares.
